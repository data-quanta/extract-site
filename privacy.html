ExTract — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 August 2026

Applies to: ExTract for iOS, version 1.0

The short version

ExTract does not collect your data. There are no accounts, no servers, no analytics, and no trackers.

Your photos are analysed entirely on your iPhone and never sent to us — because there is nowhere to

send them. We cannot see your photos, your face data, or who you are looking for.

What ExTract does with your photos

When you pick sample photos and scan your library, everything happens on your device:

  • Face detection uses Apple's on-device Vision framework.
  • Face matching uses a machine-learning model bundled inside the app itself.
  • Nothing is uploaded for processing. The app makes no network requests at all.

The app reads your photo library only after you grant permission in iOS, and only to compare faces

against the person you chose.

Background preparation. So that searching isn't a long wait, ExTract may ask iOS to prepare your

library in the background while your phone is idle — usually while it charges. This does the same

on-device work described above and stores the same on-device results; it never uploads anything, and

iOS decides if and when it runs. It is not tied to any particular person: it simply means a later

search is fast.

What is stored, and where

Everything ExTract stores stays on your device (and, if you enable it, in your own iCloud):

WhatWhereWhy
Saved people (a name, a small face image, and numeric face "embeddings")App's private storage on your iPhoneSo you can re-scan for someone later without picking photos again
Face embedding cache (numbers derived from your photos)App's cache folder on your iPhoneSo repeat scans are fast. Cleared if you delete the app, and iOS may clear it automatically
Archived photosYour own iCloud Drive, under ExTract › Archive › <person> — or on your iPhone if iCloud Drive is offSo archived photos are browsable in the Files app and sync between your devices

Face embeddings are lists of numbers describing facial features. They stay on your device and are

never transmitted.

Important: archived photos go to *your* iCloud account, provided by Apple under Apple's terms. They

do not pass through, and are not accessible to, the developer of ExTract. Deleting the app does not

delete them — remove them in the app, or in the Files app, if you want them gone.

What we collect

Nothing. Specifically, ExTract contains:

  • No analytics or telemetry
  • No advertising or ad identifiers
  • No crash-reporting SDK
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No account, login, or email capture
  • No network connections initiated by the app

For Apple's privacy label, this is "Data Not Collected."

Deleting photos

When you choose to delete photos, ExTract asks iOS to delete them and iOS shows its own confirmation —

the app cannot delete anything silently. Deleted photos go to Recently Deleted in the Photos app

and are recoverable there for 30 days, per Apple's normal behaviour.

Archiving never deletes anything: it copies the photo into a folder and leaves your original in place.

Face recognition and the law

ExTract performs face matching on your device to find photos of a person *you* select from *your own*

photo library. The resulting face data is never shared with the developer or any third party, is not

used to identify strangers, and is not used for advertising, profiling, or any purpose other than the

search you asked for.

If you are in a jurisdiction with specific biometric-data rules (for example Illinois' BIPA, or the

EU/UK GDPR's rules on biometric data), note that the developer never receives or processes your

biometric data — it is created, used, and stored solely on your own device, under your control, and you

can erase it at any time by deleting a saved person or deleting the app.

Children

ExTract is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone. Since it

collects nothing at all, there is no children's data for us to hold.

Your control

  • Delete a saved person: in the app, open Saved people → the person → ⋯ → Delete.
  • Delete archived photos: delete the person's folder in the app, or in Files › iCloud Drive › ExTract.
  • Revoke photo access: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos → ExTract.
  • Erase everything: delete the app. That removes saved people and the cache. Archived photos in your

iCloud Drive remain until you delete them yourself.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above changes with it. Material changes will be noted

in the app's release notes.
